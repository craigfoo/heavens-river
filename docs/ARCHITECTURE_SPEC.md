# Quinlan Architecture Spec (replaces / extends README 7.1 and 12)

Every rule is tagged so it stays traceable:

- **[C] Canon:** stated in the book, the author's blog, or the wiki.
- **[D] Derived:** follows from canon biology, tech level or environment.
- **[A] Artistic:** a choice made for the game; change freely.

When canon is found during a reread (see section 10), upgrade the tag and cite the chapter.

## Decisions

Made while adopting this spec; the sections below are amended to match (look for *Decided*).

- **The wet threshold is shown from outside only.** Buildings have no interiors; the threshold is what can be seen and walked round outside: slipways, water doors, a wet stone apron with a drain, gear pegs and mats under the eaves. (Section 3.)
- **Towns keep the organic walking layout** (neighbourhood commons, winding paths, rings of lanes; see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`) rather than a street parallel to the river with perpendicular alleys. Water access comes from lanes down to the quay and canal walks, each ending at a slipway. (Section 8.)
- **No carts.** Quinlans walk; main paths are for foot traffic only. (Section 2.)
- **The waterfront keeps its continuous stone quay.** The first row of buildings faces the river across it, and the quay itself is their wet threshold: each has a slipway down the quay wall in front of it. (Sections 3 and 8.)
- **Town names are invented** (syllable table), never taken from the book; the README's experience goals now say so. (Section 11.)

## 1. Design drivers

Every building decision should trace back to one of these.

| # | Driver | Source | Architectural consequence |
|---|--------|--------|---------------------------|

| 6 | Art on everything; public sculpture and murals | [C] | Decoration is structural to the look, not a garnish. Budget for it in the kit. |
| 7 | Singers (two melodies at once), loud, social | [C] | Acoustic halls, open-air performance spaces, tavern culture. |
| 8 | Agriculture and trade economy, coins | [C] | Quays, warehouses, markets, mills dominate towns. |
| 9 | Light source directly overhead on the axis | [C] | Shadows short most of the day. Shade comes from overhangs, awnings, pergolas, not orientation. |
| 10 | Weather exists (rain) | [C] | Pitched roofs, gutters, spouts, covered walkways. |

| 12 | Scatterings move populations between sections | [C] | Style is broadly shared across sections. Variation is regional flavor, not different cultures. |

## 3. The wet threshold (signature feature)

Every waterfront building has this sequence. This is the single most Quinlan idea in the spec. [D]

```
river -> slipway / underwater door -> DRIP ROOM -> DRYING ROOM -> dry interior
```

- **Slipway:** worn stone ramp into the water, 1 to 2 m wide, carved edges polished smooth by use.
- **Underwater door (optional):** leads to a half-flooded entry pool inside the foundation.
- **Drip room:** stone floor sloped to a central drain, pegs for gear, woven mats. Wet floor texture, puddles.
- **Drying room:** next to the hearth or oven, hanging cloths, warm light. Doubles as the social front room.
- **Dry interior:** timber floors, rugs, sleeping platforms.

Street-side entrance is secondary. For many buildings the street door is the back door.

*Decided:* shown from outside only (buildings have no interiors). Outside, the sequence reads as: a slipway down the quay or canal wall in front of the building (sometimes an underwater door in the wall beside it), then a drip porch at the front door (a stone apron sloped to a drain, gear on pegs, woven mats, a lean-to or deep eave over it), then the door. The back door opens on the lane behind.

## 5. Form language

- **Low and wide.** 1 to 2 floors typical, 3 max outside civic buildings. [D]
- **Rounded corners and burrow forms.** Half-sunken bank dwellings with turf roofs, especially in hamlets. Otter holts and beaver lodges are the ancestral form; urban buildings keep echoes of it in rounded door heads and domed roofs. [A]
- **Deep overhangs, 0.8 to 1.5 m,** because the light is overhead. Awnings and pergolas over markets and quays. [D]
- **Multiple exits.** Every building has at least two (land and water). Plazas have no dead-end corners. [D]
- **Lookouts.** Small rooftop platforms or towers on larger buildings; river watch posts at bends. [D]
- **Horizontal emphasis.** Long bands of windows, wide eaves, ridge lines parallel to the river. [D] *Decided:* ridges run parallel to the river on the waterfront rows; elsewhere they run along the path a house faces.
- **Patina.** Held static for a very long time means old: worn steps, repaired walls with mismatched stone, layered paint, moss at the waterline. [D]

## 6. Decoration system

Canon says everything is decorated; make that parametric.

**Density parameter `deco` 0..1** per building, from wealth and district. Even a fishing shed is never 0. Floor at 0.2. [C/A]

| Slot | Low deco | High deco |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Door frame | painted band | carved relief, figures, inlay |
| Shutters | single color | painted patterns, cut-outs |
| Roof ridge | plain | carved finials, ceramic creatures |
| Threshold | worn stone | mosaic |
| Wall | limewash | full mural |
| Dock posts | notched | carved figure posts |
| Ironwork | plain | scrolled, gilded |

**Motifs** [A]: water and flow (waves, spirals, braided channels), fish and river creatures, Quinlan figures swimming and singing, a river as an endless line (a nod to the loop they cannot see).

**Palette** [A]: ochre, terracotta, river blue, reed green, bone white, gold leaf on civic buildings. Weathered, sun-faded on upper surfaces, darker and mossy near water.

**Statues**: every square gets at least one; quays get figure posts. [C/A]

**Murals**: largest flat walls in town (warehouses, civic halls) get painted. Store a small set of mural textures and assign by seed. [C/A]

## 8. Settlement layout rules

- River is the main street. First row of buildings sits on the quay with wet thresholds. [D]
- ~~Second row faces a street parallel to the river; alleys run perpendicular down to slipways every 20 to 40 m so every home reaches water in under a minute.~~ [D] *Decided:* the town behind the waterfront row stays an organic walking town (neighbourhood commons, winding paths, rings of lanes). For water access, neighbourhoods beside the water get lanes down to the quay or a canal walk, each ending at a slipway; canal walks have slipways along them.
- Canals on flat ground loop back to the river so there is flow (no stagnant dead ends). [A]
- Public swimming pools and fountains in squares; open channels in the pavement. [D]
- Market square adjoins the main dock. [D]
- Loud uses (taverns, singing hall) cluster together near the market. [A]
- Mills and smithies downstream of housing. [D]
- Fields, orchards and reed beds outside; drying racks and fish racks on the edge. [D]
- Crossing points: stepping stones in hamlets, timber bridges in towns, arched stone bridges in cities with swimmer clearance. [D]

## 9. Generation parameters (suggested)

```ts
interface BuildingParams {
  type: BuildingType;
  floors: 1 | 2 | 3;
  deco: number;          // 0.2..1
  roof: 'thatch' | 'turf' | 'tile' | 'shingle' | 'slate' | 'dome';
  base: 'riverstone' | 'fieldstone' | 'ashlar';
  upper: 'daub' | 'timberPlaster' | 'stone';
  wetThreshold: boolean; // true for all waterfront
  exits: number;         // >= 2
  age: number;           // 0..1, drives patina, repairs, moss
  palette: number;       // index into palette table
  lookout: boolean;
}
```

Derive `deco`, `roof`, `base`, `upper` from settlement tier + district wealth + local biome. Derive `age` from distance to town center (oldest at the quay).
